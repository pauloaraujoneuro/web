## Facts and where they come from

1. Three kinds of injury, in BSSH's patient wording `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source A):
   - bruised nerve: stops working for a short time and "normally recovers fully";
   - damage to the inner fibres with the outer layer intact: recovery "more unpredictable";
   - cut nerve: needs repair to recover; after repair some permanent loss of function remains.
2. Regrowth speed ≈ 1 mm/day; nerves take many months; final result may not be
   known for two years or more `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source A).
3. Recovery tends to be less complete with older age `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source A).
4. Without a wound: watchful waiting with nerve conduction studies/EMG; with a
   wound and signs of nerve injury: operation to inspect the nerve; grafts when
   damage is long or there is no sign of healing `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (Source A).
5. Time matters most for motor recovery because chronically denervated muscle
   loses its ability to be reinnervated; observation is reasonable when
   spontaneous recovery is plausible and serial examination shows improvement
   `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (evidence-pack S10, S11).
6. Reinnervation time depends on distance to the target muscle, severity, age,
   target condition and rehabilitation `[EXPERT-OPINION]` (evidence-pack S11, S24).
7. EMG/NCS help document denervation and reinnervation, but timing after injury
   affects interpretation; serial examination matters (evidence-pack §Diagnosis).

## Do not state

- That 1 mm/day converts into a personal recovery date (evidence-pack rule).
- Any cure promise or success percentage.
- That every nerve injury needs surgery, or that waiting is always safe.
- That tingling/Tinel alone proves recovery (BSSH lists Tinel as a sign of injury, not of regrowth).
